How to use: Use Next to step through each stage, or Play to watch the whole process automatically. The dots below the buttons let you jump to any stage. Watch what happens to the chromosomes — they copy themselves, line up at the middle, then pull apart. Two identical daughter cells come out the other end.
Notice: the chromosomes copy themselves before the cell divides — during interphase, when you can't even see them. Then during anaphase they get pulled apart to opposite ends. Each daughter cell ends up with the same number and type of chromosomes as the parent. That's why mitosis produces identical clones — it's used for growth and repair, not for making sex cells.
