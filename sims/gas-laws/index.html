How to use: The cylinder holds a fixed amount of gas at a constant temperature, so only two things can change: volume and pressure. Drag the piston in — the same gas squeezed into a smaller space bumps the walls harder, and the pressure readout climbs. Pull it out and the pressure drops. Watch the moving point trace the curve on the graph.
Notice: halving the volume doubles the pressure — that's the whole of Boyle's law, P × V = a constant. Look at the readout: no matter where the piston sits, the product P × V stays the same because the temperature hasn't moved. It's why a sealed aerosol can in a fire explodes and why divers rising too fast get the bends.
