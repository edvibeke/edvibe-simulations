How to use: Blood flows continuously through the heart, day and night. Blue blood is deoxygenated — it comes from the body via the vena cava, goes through the right side of the heart, and is pumped to the lungs via the pulmonary artery. Red blood is oxygenated — it comes back from the lungs via the pulmonary vein, goes through the left side, and is pumped to the whole body via the aorta. The two sides never mix.
Notice: the two sides of the heart are completely separate — that's why it's called double circulation. The right side sends blood to the lungs (a short trip). The left side sends blood to the whole body (a long trip) — which is why the left ventricle's wall is much thicker and more muscular. It has to pump harder. Blue blood is never red blood — they don't mix.
